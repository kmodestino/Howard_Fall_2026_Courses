# Oral Defense

The oral defense is a one-on-one conversation with me about a text of your choosing, worth 3 points. It tests how well you know that text: whether you understand it well enough to make a substantial claim about it, can find the evidence that supports the claim, and can respond to questions that put pressure on it.

## Instructions

- **Scheduling.** Schedule a meeting with me whenever you are ready, at any point in the semester during my office hours. Use the booking page linked here: [[About Me]]. Most of these should be done by Thanksgiving Break, but I am able to do a few the week following Thanksgiving. Check with me if you need to do it then, it will be trickier to schedule, but we will make it work.
- **Choosing a text.** Your text must be something we have read this semester. Choose one you know well enough to talk about without notes doing the work for you.
- **What to prepare.** Come with one substantial interpretive claim about the text: an argument about what it means or what it is doing, not a summary of what happens in it. Know where in the text your evidence is. Bring the text with you. Avoid just saying "the text shows/demonstrates/displays etc..." (and other vague verbs that often introduce a summary of themes). A contestable claim will often use more active and specific verbs, not vague metaphorical ones.
- **What happens.** You state your claim briefly and point to the passages that support it. I then ask a few questions that press on the claim. Answer from the text. Holding your claim, qualifying it, or refining it in response to a good objection can all earn full credit, as long as your answer shows you know the text.

## Rubric

Each criterion is worth 1 point, with half points possible, for 3 points total.

| Criterion                                                        | 1 point                                                                                                                                                                      | ½ point                                                                                                                                            | 0 points                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Claim:** understanding sufficient to say something substantial | The claim could only come from someone who has understood the text. It addresses what the text means or is doing, not just what happens, and is specific enough to be wrong. | The claim rests on an accurate grasp of the text but stays general: true of this text, but of many others too, or close to summary.                | No claim, or a claim built on a misreading of the text's basic content.        |
| **Evidence:** finding what bears on the claim                    | Points to particular passages that support the claim and explains how they support it. Can find their way around the text when asked.                                        | Evidence is relevant but loosely tied to the claim: passages are cited as illustrations rather than read, or support is hard to locate when asked. | Little or no textual evidence, or evidence that does not bear on the claim.    |
| **Response under pressure:** answering a few questions           | Answers from the text. Engages each pressure point, then holds, qualifies, or refines the claim in a way that shows further understanding.                                   | Handles some questions well but falls back on repetition, generalities, or abandoning the claim on others.                                         | Cannot engage the questions, or the answers show the reading does not hold up. |
