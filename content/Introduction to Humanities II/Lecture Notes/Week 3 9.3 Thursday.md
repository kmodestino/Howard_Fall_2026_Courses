## Key Outcomes

The class explored _Do the Right Thing_ as an opportunity for moral inquiry, focusing on Mookie’s decision to throw the trash barrel through Sal’s pizzeria window. The discussion emphasized that evaluating the action requires more than judging its consequences; it also requires examining Mookie’s purpose, deliberation, character, social circumstances, and the historical context of racial injustice. 123

## Moral Frameworks Comparison

- **Consequentialism**: Evaluates actions primarily according to their outcomes, such as property damage, preventing further violence, or producing longer-term political consequences.
    
- **Deontological Ethics**: Judges actions according to universal rules and standards, including the categorical imperative.
    
- **Aristotelian Ethics**: Treats morality as a practical judgment made within concrete situations. This framework focuses on the agent’s purpose, knowledge, character, and circumstances rather than on consequences alone. 123
    

## Key Aristotelian Concepts

- **Telos**: The internal purpose or aim guiding an action, distinct from its external consequence.
    
- **Voluntary, Involuntary, and Mixed Actions**: Mookie’s action originates within Mookie but occurs under the pressure of racial tension, police violence, and the threat of further harm. For this reason, the action can be understood as a mixed action.
    
- **Prohairesis**: Deliberate choice involving the selection of means to achieve a desired end. Mookie’s pause before throwing the barrel suggests deliberation rather than a purely passion-driven reaction.
    
- **Hexis**: A state of character formed through repeated habits and practices. Mookie’s everyday relationship with the community provides part of the context for interpreting the action. 12
    

## Application to Mookie’s Action

The class considered several possible interpretations of Mookie’s purpose. The action may have expressed solidarity with the community, redirected the crowd’s anger from people toward property, or prevented further violence against Sal and Sal’s son. Students also debated whether Mookie’s decision represented a calculated attempt to limit harm or a passionate “crash-out” moment. 123

The discussion also addressed how focusing primarily on the destruction of the pizzeria can obscure Radio Raheem’s death and the broader injustice surrounding the event. The question of whether Mookie “did the right thing” therefore reveals the moral frameworks and historical assumptions audiences bring to the film. 23

## Historical Context of the Film

The instructor emphasized that Spike Lee embeds historical references throughout the film, providing the “weight of history” necessary for understanding the community’s response:

- **Michael Stewart (1983)**: A Black man who died after being arrested and brutalized in NYPD custody.
    
- **Howard Beach (1986)**: A racial attack against three Black men that resulted in Michael Griffith’s death.
    
- **Tawana Brawley (1987)**: A case involving allegations of abduction and abuse that became associated with racial injustice and media character assassination. 13
