## Metaphorical Analysis Framework

The instructor outlined five specific functions of metaphor that distinguish it from formal argument 3:

- Exceeds Intent: Metaphors often say more than the speaker consciously means, inviting broader interpretation 7.
- Simultaneous Opposites: They can signify contradictory ideas at once, such as justice and injustice 8.
- Temporal Accumulation: Meaning is not singular but accrues over the course of a work 9.
- Reveals Underlying Agreement: Opponents using the same metaphors may share a common conceptual frame despite their disagreements 10.
- Anchors in the Physical World: Metaphors bring social, economic, and class-based realities into abstract discussions 411.

## Application to Aeschylus

The "net" metaphor was traced through several key plot points to illustrate the nature of retribution 12:

- Divine Justice: The chorus invokes Zeus's "gigantic toils of enslavement" to justify the total destruction of Troy as a payment for Paris stealing Helen 1213.
- Foreshadowing: Clytemnestra uses the image of a "fishing net" to describe imagined wounds to Agamemnon, which foreshadows her actual use of a net of robes to entrap and kill him 1415.
- Cycle of Violence: The net represents a "hunter's justice" that is indiscriminate and captures everyone, regardless of individual guilt 2.
- Interpersonal Vengeance: The discussion highlighted the lack of a formal court system in the play's world, leaving only "eye for an eye" violence 16.
