## Core Concepts Introduced

Aristotle's Definition of Tragedy (Poetics, Ch. 6)

- Tragedy = imitation (mimesis) of a serious, complete action of certain magnitude, in dramatic form, evoking pity and fear leading to catharsis (purgation). 56
- Key distinction: tragedy imitates actions, not persons—e.g., Julius Caesar is about Brutus's act of assassination, not Caesar's biography. 78

Close Reading

- Attending to syntax, word choice, and structure to make evidence-based claims about meaning. 910
- Skill practiced throughout the semester; supplementary chapter Close Reading for the 21st Century provided. 11

Hermeneutics

- Moving between a text and its broader historical/interpretive context to build the best available account—neither purely objective nor purely subjective. 1213
- Concept of fusion of horizons (Gadamer): good interpretation merges the text's horizon with the reader's own. 13

## Discussion Commitments
