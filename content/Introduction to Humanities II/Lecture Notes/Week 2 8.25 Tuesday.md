We watched the first half of Do the Right Things and had a discussion. The following notes are collated from both sections.

## Narrative Structure

- Unified vs. episodic tension: Most students agreed the film feels episodic early on but becomes increasingly unified as storylines intersect and tensions escalate. 12
- Mood as structure: The film builds like a "crescendo" — shifting from microaggressions to macroaggressions as runtime increases, with the emotional arc serving as the unifying thread rather than individual plot lines. 3
- Vignette quality: Some students described early scenes as distinct vignettes sharing one overarching theme, comparable to a single performance with multiple acts. 2

## Key Themes Identified

- Double consciousness: Mookie's behavior — de-escalating conflicts rather than confronting racism directly — was read as an example of W.E.B. Du Bois's concept of double consciousness, navigating identity as both American and Black American. 45
- "Do the right thing" as ambiguous: The title's meaning shifts by perspective — moral good, business self-interest, or law enforcement justification — making it a justice question without a single answer. 6
- Gentrification: The white jogger in a Larry Bird Celtics shirt walking through a Black Brooklyn neighborhood was flagged as an early, pointed signal of gentrification, reflecting Spike Lee's own experience in Fort Greene. 78
- Whose space is public: Radio Raheem's boombox was read as a deliberate claim of public presence, contrasted with Mookie's more deferential, laid-back posture in the community. 9

## Symbolism

- The heat: Repeatedly cited as a pressure-cooker metaphor — the sweltering day amplifies every frustration and accelerates the boiling-over of racial tensions. 1011
- We Love Radio / Senior Love Daddy: The DJ (played by Samuel Jackson) was proposed as the film's "chorus" — a narrative voice articulating the overarching theme of love amid conflict. 112
- Sal's property rights: Sal's repeated assertion of ownership over the pizzeria as a recurring flashpoint — the tension between who the space serves and who controls it. 13

## Historical & Cultural Context

- Ethnic enclaves: The film's segregated-yet-adjacent communities mirror real patterns in New York and the Northeast, where towns cluster by ethnicity despite geographic proximity. 1014
- 1989 context: the film's pressure-cooker atmosphere connected to the Rodney King beating and LA uprising — not far off historically — as part of a broader pattern of urban ethnic tension. 15
- Internalized racism: Sal's family serving an almost entirely Black and POC neighborhood while displaying hostility toward that same community was identified as a clear example of apparent or internalized racism. 16

## Viewing Focus for Next Session

- Track how conflicts are initiated and whether/how they get resolved — particularly the social rituals (e.g., the boombox volume standoff) that defuse or fail to defuse tension. 13
- Continue identifying the "chorus" voice and what it reveals about the film's moral argument. 12
