# Course Policies: Attendance, Participation, and Oral Assessment

---
## How Your Grade Works: Criteria-Based Assessment

Your grade in this course uses criteria-based assessment rather than assignment-based grading. I am not collecting a stack of individually graded papers, quizzes, or exercises and averaging them. Instead, you are assessed on whether you meet a set of explicit, predetermined criteria — for attendance, for the quality of your oral contributions in class, and for your performance in the one-on-one defense. Each criterion is defined in the sections below, with a clear threshold for what counts as meeting it. Your grade is a direct function of which criteria you meet, not an average of graded artifacts.

Attendance, oral participation, and the one-on-one defense together account for up to 20 points of your final grade:

- **Attendance** — up to 3 points
- **Oral participation (disputatio)** — up to 14 points
- **One-on-one oral defense** — up to 3 points

These 20 points convert directly to your final letter grade for the course:

|Points|Grade|
|---|---|
|18–20|A|
|16–17|B|
|14–15|C|
|12–13|D|
|0–11|F|

## I. Attendance

Regular attendance is required and is treated as a baseline condition of the course, not an optional courtesy. This is not merely administrative: class attendance is, across the empirical literature, the single strongest predictor of final grades in college courses — a stronger predictor than SAT scores, high school GPA, or self-reported study habits. Mandatory attendance policies show a modest but real positive effect on grades and disproportionately benefit students who are otherwise at risk of failing.

**Policy:**

- Fewer than three absences: 3 points added to your final grade.
- Three to five absences: 2 points added to your final grade.
- More than five absences: 0 points added, and the final course grade is capped at a C, regardless of performance on other coursework.
- Absences connected to a documented disability, religious observance, medical emergency, or university-sanctioned activity are handled through accommodation and do not count against the above thresholds; raise these with the instructor as early as possible.

**Punctuality:** Students are expected to be logged in, camera-ready, and prepared to begin at the scheduled start time. Arriving more than 15 minutes late on three occasions will be treated as one unexcused absence.

## II. Conduct in Synchronous (Online) Sessions

This course meets synchronously online, which places different demands on students than a self-paced module. Live discussion depends on everyone being genuinely present — not just connected. The following are course requirements, not suggestions:

**1. Cameras on.** Students are expected to have their video on for the duration of class. This is a discussion-based course, and I teach by reading the room — I cannot do that with a grid of black boxes. If a student anticipates a problem meeting this expectation, they should speak with me individually; this is not something to be negotiated in the abstract or opted out of by default.

**2. A quiet, distraction-free environment.** Join from a space where you can hear and be heard, without significant background noise or movement. You should be seated, not walking, driving, or in a public/high-traffic space. If you are driving or in an otherwise unsafe location for looking at a screen, you will be asked to leave the course and marked as absent.

**3. Full attention.** No phones, no browsing, no working on other coursework during class. Divided attention undermines comprehension and retention, and it is also disruptive to discussion-based teaching, which depends on students actually tracking the conversation as it unfolds.

**4. The reading, done, before class.** Come having read the assigned material and prepared to reference specific passages. This is a discussion-based course; it does not function if students are reading for the first time in real time. Unprepared students should expect to be called on and to have that reflected in participation credit.

**Accommodation note:** Any student for whom one of these expectations is genuinely unworkable (unreliable internet, a shared or unsafe living space, a disability-related need) should contact the instructor to arrange an alternative _before_ it becomes a pattern, not after.

## III. Oral Participation: The Disputatio Model (14 points)

This course assesses participation orally rather than through response papers, reading quizzes, or discussion-board posts. Each session I will lecture on the assigned material; the floor then opens to students. Written fluency about a text is not a substitute for being able to think with it in real time, and this format also makes it very difficult to substitute someone else's thinking (including an AI's) for your own.

Participation is not measured by simply speaking. Your questions and contributions — about the reading, the lecture, or the concepts under discussion — should demonstrate genuine engagement along at least two of the following three modes:

1. **Interpretation** — an actual reading of the text or concept: an argument about what it means, not just a report of what it says.
2. **Use of evidence** — grounding the claim in something specific: a passage, a detail, a particular point from the lecture.
3. **Contextualization or comparison** — situating the material against something else: another text, a historical circumstance, a concept from earlier in the course.

Each two-week period, you should be prepared to demonstrate at least two of these three modes at some point in discussion:

- **2 points** — At some point during the period, a contribution demonstrates at least two of the three modes.
- **1 point** — A contribution demonstrates only one of the three modes, or engagement is present but thin.
- **0 points** — No meaningful verbal contribution during the two-week period.

Over the course of the semester this yields 14 possible points.

**Note: If you're unable to find space in class to participate, you may earn your points for a given two-week period by scheduling a one-on-one or small-group discussion with me. This must take place within one week after the two-week period ends, and should involve presenting some of your ideas about the reading from that period.

**A note on record-keeping:** To make sure I can give you full and accurate credit for your in-class contributions, I use AI-generated transcription (via Zoom or Notion) of class sessions to help track participation. Please make sure the name displayed under your video/username is your actual name — not a nickname, initials, or a blank field — so your contributions are correctly attributed in the record.

## IV. One-on-One Oral Defense (3 points)

In the second half of the semester, each student will meet individually with the instructor to discuss one reading of their own choosing in depth. The student selects the text, comes prepared to make a short, spoken interpretive claim about it, and must be ready to answer questions and defend that interpretation under scrutiny. This is not a recitation of secondary opinion; it is a test of whether the student has actually developed and can hold a reading of their own. I will be distributing a rubric once we reach the midpoint of the semester.
