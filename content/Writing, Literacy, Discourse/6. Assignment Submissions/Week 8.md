# Evidence/Analysis Paragraph

**DUE Oct 15 Midnight
**Due:** one full body paragraph drafted from your own essay, built around substantial evidence and analysis, with quotations cited in MLA format.

**What this means:**  
This assignment asks you to write one complete paragraph from the essay you're working on. The paragraph should make a claim and develop it through several pieces of evidence, each followed by your analysis. The goal is a paragraph where the quotations and your thinking about them do most of the work, rather than summary or general statements. This paragraph should be one you expect to use (and revise) in your first draft.

Three terms matter here:

- **Integrating a quotation** means bringing the quoted words into a sentence of your own instead of dropping them in by themselves. You can introduce the quotation with a signal phrase ("Douglass writes that...") or weave a short phrase from the text into your own grammar.
- **Analysis** means explaining what the quoted language shows and why it supports your claim. Good analysis looks closely at particular words, images, or phrasing, rather than restating the quotation in other words.
- **MLA format** is the citation system we'll use for the essay. It has two parts that work together:
    - An **in-text citation** in parentheses after the quotation, giving the author's last name and the page number: (Douglass 58). If you've already named the author in your sentence, give only the page: (58). The period goes _after_ the parentheses, not inside the quotation marks. If your source is an online text without page numbers, leave the page out, or use a chapter or paragraph number if the text has them: (Douglass, ch. 10), or (ch. 10) if you've named him in the sentence.
    - A **Works Cited** entry at the end of your paper, listing the full source so a reader can find it. The in-text citation points to this entry.

**Example:**

> Douglass presents his fight with Covey as the moment his identity is reversed, telling the reader, "You have seen how a man was made a slave; you shall see how a slave was made a man" (ch. 10).

Matching Works Cited entry:

> Douglass, Frederick. _Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, an American Slave_. 1845. _Project Gutenberg_, [www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/23](http://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/23).

**Instructions:**

- At the top of the page, copy your current working thesis.
- Choose one claim from your essay that supports that thesis, and open the paragraph with a sentence stating it.
- Integrate at least two quotations from the text(s) your essay analyzes, each with an MLA in-text citation.
- After each quotation, write at least two sentences of analysis connecting it back to your claim.
- End with a sentence that connects the paragraph's claim back to your thesis.
- Below the paragraph, include a Works Cited entry for each source you quoted.
