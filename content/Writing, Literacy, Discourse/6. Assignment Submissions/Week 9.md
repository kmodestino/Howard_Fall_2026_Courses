**Week 9 — Structural Outline**  
**Due: Today, Wednesday, October 14, by 6:30 p.m.**

**What this means:**  
A structural outline (or reverse outline) lists one line per paragraph, stating what each paragraph _does_ (its job) rather than what it _says_ (its content). “Introduces the claim,” “presents the strongest evidence,” “raises and answers the counterargument,” “closes by returning to the opening image.” This lets you see your essay’s shape the way an X-ray shows a skeleton instead of skin.

**A note on timing and drafts in progress:**  
Your essay is still a work in progress, and this outline should reflect that. You don’t need to know exactly how every paragraph will turn out. Some lines will describe paragraphs you’ve already written; others will describe paragraphs you expect or hope to write. Speculation is fine, and even useful: guessing at a paragraph’s job now is a way of finding out whether your essay needs it. If you’re unsure about a paragraph, write your best guess and add a question mark.

This is meant to be quick. You’ll have class time to start, and it should take no more than about two hours total. I’m assigning it now because it pushes you to think about your whole essay while you still have plenty of time before the draft is due. You shouldn’t be writing the essay all at the last minute. Instead, keep thinking about it and spend about ten minutes on it every day. The longer it stays an active project in your mind, the more ideas you’ll have and the easier it will be to work on.

**Instructions:**

- Write one line per paragraph you plan to include. If you’ve already started drafting, write one line per paragraph you’ve written, plus lines for the ones still to come.
- Each line states the paragraph’s function, not its content.
- Aim for 6–10 lines.
- Mark any paragraph you’re unsure about with a question mark.
