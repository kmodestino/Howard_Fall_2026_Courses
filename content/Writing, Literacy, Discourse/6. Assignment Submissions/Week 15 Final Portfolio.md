# Final Portfolio

Due: Sunday, December 6, at 11:59 p.m.

## What this is

The final portfolio collects five pieces of writing from this semester, revised in light of what you learned after you first wrote them. Four are pieces you have already drafted. The fifth, the cover letter, you write last.

## The five pieces, in this order

### 1. Sentence exercise

The exercise from Week 2 on Parataxis and Hypotaxis. Revised if needed.

Original Assignment: [[Week 2]]
### 2. Subordinate paragraph

Your Week 3 paragraph exercise. Revised if needed.

Original Assignment: [[Week 3]]
### 3. Claim-and-evidence paragraph

Your Evidence/Analysis Paragraph from Week 8.

Original Assignment: [[Week 8]]


### 4. Essay

Your five-page interpretive essay, revised from the first draft you submitted on October 25. This is the centerpiece of the portfolio.

- Thesis: one specific, contestable sentence in the first paragraph. It still passes both tests from Candidate Theses: two careful readers could disagree about what the evidence means, and you are interpreting specific things you can read closely.
- Evidence: each quotation or detail is introduced and followed by at least two sentences of analysis connecting it back to your claim.
- Structure and transitions: every paragraph has a job you could name in one line, and the end of each paragraph and the start of the next show why the second follows from the first. Paragraphs should fail the swap test from your Transition Skeleton.
- Format: five full pages not counting Works Cited, 12-point Times New Roman or similar, double-spaced throughout, one-inch margins, MLA in-text citations and a Works Cited page.

### 5. Reflective cover letter

One to two pages, addressed to me, in your own voice, explaining what changed across your drafts and why.

- For each of pieces 1 through 4, name at least one specific revision you made and explain why you made it.
- For the essay, say how your thesis changed from Candidate Theses to the final version.
- Write the letter last, after the rest of the portfolio is complete.

## How the portfolio is graded

Each piece is checked against one plain standard and is either complete (full points) or not yet complete (0 points, revise and resubmit by Week 14, no penalty). Pieces are not scored on a quality scale or averaged.

|                                 |                                                                         |        |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Portfolio Piece                 | Specifications for Complete                                             | Points |
| 1. Sentence-level exercise      | Three imitation sentences, revised once.                                | 2      |
| 2. Subordinating Paragraph      | One paragraph with a topic sentence and developed subordination.        | 2      |
| 3. Claim-and-evidence paragraph | A specific claim supported by one integrated quotation and analysis.    | 3      |
| 4. Full structured essay        | A complete, revised essay with a sustained claim and visible structure. | 4      |
| 5. Reflective cover letter      | A short letter connecting the four pieces to the course's threads.      | 3      |

Portfolio total: 14 points.
