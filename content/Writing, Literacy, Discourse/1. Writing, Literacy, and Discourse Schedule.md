## Weekly Schedule

### Week 1: Introduction — In Class Only

**Day 1:** Syllabus and course overview; 
     ---Helpful supplement: [[Close Reading Intro.pdf|Sinykin and Winant "Close Reading for the 21st Century]]

**Day 2:** Anne Bradstreet, "The Author to Her Book" (1666) — [https://poets.org/poem/author-her-book](https://poets.org/poem/author-her-book)

**Weekly writing activity:** Nothing due.

### Week 2: Sentence — Syntax as Resource

_Threads: Introducing: Sentence._

**Primary:** Mary Rowlandson, Narrative of the Captivity and Restoration (1682) — [https://www.gutenberg.org/files/851/851-h/851-h.htm](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/851/851-h/851-h.htm) _(read the first two 'Removes')_

**Paired:** Powhatan, Speech to Captain John Smith (1609) — [https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/nativeamericans/chiefpowhatan.htm](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/nativeamericans/chiefpowhatan.htm)

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: three original sentences that imitate a cumulative-sentence pattern (base clause plus free modifiers).

### Week 3: Paragraph — Coherence and Escalation

_Threads: Reinforcing: Sentence. Introducing: Paragraph._

**Primary:** Jonathan Edwards, Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God (1741) — [https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1053&context=etas](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1053&context=etas) 

**Paired:** Phillis Wheatley, "On Being Brought from Africa to America" (1773) — [https://poets.org/poem/being-brought-africa-america](https://poets.org/poem/being-brought-africa-america)

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: one paragraph — a topic sentence followed by three levels of subordinate sentences.

### Week 4: Paragraph as Argument / Claims Introduced

_Threads: Reinforcing: Paragraph. Introducing: Claims._

**Primary:** Thomas Paine, Common Sense (1776) — [https://www.ushistory.org/paine/commonsense/](https://www.ushistory.org/paine/commonsense/) _(excerpt: the opening argument against monarchy)_

**Paired:** The Declaration of Independence (1776) — [https://www.ushistory.org/declaration/document/](https://www.ushistory.org/declaration/document/)

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: one paragraph identifying a claim, its supporting data, and its underlying warrant.

### Week 5: Claims — Radical and Comparative

_Threads: Reinforcing: Paragraph, Sentence. Introducing: Claims (full unit)._

**Primary:** David Walker, Appeal to the Coloured Citizens of the World (1829) — [https://www.gutenberg.org/files/16516/16516-h/16516-h.htm](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/16516/16516-h/16516-h.htm) _(excerpt from Article I or the Preamble)_

**Paired:** Red Jacket, "Religion for the White Man, Not the Red Man" (1805) — [https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/speech-of-red-jacket/](https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/speech-of-red-jacket/)

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: three candidate thesis claims, each with one sentence of data and one sentence of warrant.

### Week 6: Claims — Irony and Structural Comparison

_Threads: Reinforcing: Claims, Sentence._

**Primary:** William Apess, "An Indian's Looking-Glass for the White Man" (1833) — [https://pressbooks.online.ucf.edu/aml3031/chapter/wiliam-apes/](https://pressbooks.online.ucf.edu/aml3031/chapter/wiliam-apes/)

**Paired:** Frederick Douglass, "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?" (1852) — [https://daily.jstor.org/what-to-the-slave-is-the-fourth-of-july-annotated/](https://daily.jstor.org/what-to-the-slave-is-the-fourth-of-july-annotated/) _(annotated full text)_

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: one claim and one paragraph of support.

### Week 7: Evidence / Analysis — Integration

_Threads: Reinforcing: Claims. Introducing: Evidence/Analysis._

**Primary:** Frederick Douglass, Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass (1845) — [https://www.gutenberg.org/files/23/23-h/23-h.htm](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/23/23-h/23-h.htm) _(the Covey fight, Chapter X)_

**Paired:** Harriet Jacobs, Incidents in the Life of a Slave Girl (1861) — [https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11030](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11030) _(excerpt from the garret chapters)_

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: one paragraph integrating a quotation with two sentences of analysis.

### Week 8: Evidence / Analysis — Two Slave Narratives

_Threads: Reinforcing: Evidence/Analysis._

**Primary:** Olaudah Equiano, The Interesting Narrative (1789) — [https://www.gutenberg.org/files/15399/15399-h/15399-h.htm](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/15399/15399-h/15399-h.htm) _(the Middle Passage chapter)_

**Paired:** Black Hawk, Life of Black Hawk (1833), dictated to Antoine LeClaire — [https://archive.org/details/lifeofblackhawkm00blacuoft](https://archive.org/details/lifeofblackhawkm00blacuoft)

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: one evidence/analysis paragraph — claim, quotation, two sentences of analysis.

### Week 9: Structure — Whole-Essay Architecture

_Threads: Reinforcing: Evidence/Analysis, Claims. Introducing: Structure._

**Primary:** Ralph Waldo Emerson, "Self-Reliance" (1841) — [https://www.gutenberg.org/files/16643/16643-h/16643-h.htm](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/16643/16643-h/16643-h.htm) 

**Paired:** Frances E. W. Harper, "Bury Me in a Free Land" (1858) — [https://poets.org/poem/bury-me-free-land](https://poets.org/poem/bury-me-free-land) 

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: a structural outline for an essay.

### Week 10: Structure — Delay and Counterargument

_Threads: Reinforcing: Structure._

**Primary:** Henry David Thoreau, "Civil Disobedience" (1849) — [https://www.gutenberg.org/files/71/71-h/71-h.htm](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/71/71-h/71-h.htm)

**Paired:** Sojourner Truth, "Ain't I a Woman?" — compare the 1851 Robinson transcript and the 1863 Gage version — [https://www.thesojournertruthproject.com/compare-the-speeches](https://www.thesojournertruthproject.com/compare-the-speeches) _(compare how each version orders its argument)_

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: a full draft of an argumentative essay (claim, evidence, counterargument, structure).

### Week 11: Rhetorical Choice — Irony and Withholding

_Threads: Reinforcing: Structure. Introducing: Rhetorical choice (as an audience-facing lens on prior units)._

**Primary:** Herman Melville, "Bartleby, the Scrivener" (1853), in The Piazza Tales — [https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15859](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15859) 

**Paired:** Edgar Allan Poe, "Ligeia" (1838), in The Works of Edgar Allan Poe, Vol. 1 — [https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2147/2147-h/2147-h.htm](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2147/2147-h/2147-h.htm)

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: one paragraph revised twice for two different readers.

### Week 12: Rhetorical Choice / Editing — Drafts as Rhetorical Choices

_Threads: Reinforcing: Rhetorical choice. Introducing: Editing._

**Primary:** Abraham Lincoln, Gettysburg Address — compare the five surviving manuscript drafts — [https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/gettysburg-address/](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/gettysburg-address/) _(Library of Congress exhibit with drafts)_

**Paired:** Walt Whitman, Preface to Leaves of Grass (1855) — [https://www.bartleby.com/109/15.html](https://www.bartleby.com/109/15.html)

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: a revised draft, with two edited passes marked.

### Week 13: Editing — Revision as Interpretation

_Threads: Reinforcing: Editing, Sentence._

**Primary:** Emily Dickinson, manuscript fascicles and variant readings — [https://www.edickinson.org/](https://www.edickinson.org/) _(browse 2–3 poems' manuscript images and compare to printed versions)_

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: a marked-up peer draft, with a short editorial note.

### Week 14: Synthesis / Portfolio

_Threads: All threads converge._

**Primary:** John Winthrop, A Model of Christian Charity (1630) — [https://www.gilderlehrman.org/sites/default/files/inline-pdfs/A%20Model%20of%20Christian%20Charity_Full%20Text.pdf](https://www.gilderlehrman.org/sites/default/files/inline-pdfs/A%20Model%20of%20Christian%20Charity_Full%20Text.pdf)

**Paired:** Frederick Douglass, "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?" — [https://daily.jstor.org/what-to-the-slave-is-the-fourth-of-july-annotated/](https://daily.jstor.org/what-to-the-slave-is-the-fourth-of-july-annotated/)

**Weekly writing activity:** Due: final portfolio and cover letter.
