## Key Outcomes

The class focused on two foundational sentence-level concepts—parataxis and hypotaxis—and applied them to Mary Rowlandson's captivity narrative. Students identified examples of both in the text and discussed how Rowlandson's stylistic choices reflect her psychological state and rhetorical purpose. The session also contextualized the text within Puritan spiritual autobiography and its lasting influence on American identity narratives.

---

## Core Concepts Introduced

- Parataxis: Clauses or phrases placed side-by-side with no explicit logical connective; leaves the reader to infer relationships. 1
    
    - Classic example: "I came, I saw, I conquered" — monumental, fast, rhythmically powerful 1
    - Effect: Creates ambiguity in temporal/causal relationships; demands more interpretive work from the reader 2
- Hypotaxis: Subordination that explicitly shows logical or causal relationships between clauses. 1
    
    - Example: "I came in order to see and conquer" — slower, more precise, logically organized 3
    - Effect: Explains rather than presents; rhetorically weaker but more precise 4
- Key distinction: Parataxis = expressive/poetic; hypotaxis = logical/explanatory. Writers choose based on whether they want readers to interpret or be guided. 4

## Application to Mary Rowlandson

- The attack sequence in the second paragraph is heavily paratactic: rapid, fragmented clauses mirror the chaos and confusion of the raid. 5
- Student observation (Zane): the fast pace of paratactic writing mirrors the "suddenness and brutality" of the attack. 6
- One sentence slows into hypotaxis — the man who begged for his life — to zoom in on a specific, grotesque scene before delivering its most disturbing detail. 78
- The final sentence of the first remove is hypotactic: reported speech, attribution, and cause-and-effect argument. 9
- Parataxis in the text carries biblical resonance: the King James Bible is stylistically dominated by parataxis, making it feel prophetic and weighty to its Puritan audience. 10

## Historical and Rhetorical Context

- Rowlandson writes in the tradition of spiritual autobiography, inaugurated by Augustine's Confessions — life narrative as a model for the community of believers. 11
- Her text is likely the first use of autobiographical writing to address a specific historical community (Massachusetts Bay Colony) rather than the abstract universal church. 12
- The Puritans framed King Philip's War not as colonial conflict but as a divine test of God's chosen people, using Rowlandson's narrative to shore up communal identity rather than question it. 1314
- This logic — crisis as proof of special status rather than ordinary failure — is identified as a root of American exceptionalism in political rhetoric. 15
- The captivity narrative genre is noted as problematic: it reverses aggressor/victim roles, obscures settler-colonial violence, and has analogues in contemporary geopolitical rhetoric. 1617

## Writing Rhythm Beyond Poetry

- Prose rhythm operates on the same principles as poetic stress: varied sentence length creates emphasis and prevents monotony. 18
- AI writing tends to over-regularize rhythm, producing artificial, flat emphasis. 18
- Even without formal meter, writers are always making rhythmic choices at the sentence level. 19
