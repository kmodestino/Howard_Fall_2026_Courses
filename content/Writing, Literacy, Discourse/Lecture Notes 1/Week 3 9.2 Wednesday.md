## Phillis Wheatley: Historical Context

- Identity: The first published African-American poet, born in West Africa and enslaved in Boston 89.
- Publication: Her work was published by her owners in London (1773) and was a bestseller in the Atlantic world, though primarily purchased by white readers 81011.
- Critical Reception: Originally viewed by some as performing subservience, recent scholarship re-evaluates her as a complex, rebellious, and creative poet 12.
- Style: A neoclassical poet of the 18th century, she utilized formal rhyme and meter rather than free verse 9.

## Rhetorical Analysis of the Poem

- Structural Units: The poem consists of three distinct sentences that shift in subjectivity from the individual ("me") to a collective race ("our") to a general theological lecture 113.
- Literary Devices:
    
    - Asyndeton: The omission of conjunctions in the first unit speeds up the narrative 14.
    - Sermocinatio: Wheatley attributes the phrase "diabolic dye" to racist critics to expose and critique their views without endorsing them 15.
    - Puns: The use of "cane" (sugar cane) and "dye" (indigo dye) references the brutal economy of slave plantations in the West Indies 16.
- Theological Argument: Wheatley usurps the role of a preacher to lecture Christians, arguing that if Cain could be redeemed, racial exclusion from Christianity is logically and biblically unsound 217.
