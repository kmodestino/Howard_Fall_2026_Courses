## How Your Grade Works: Criteria-Based Assessment

Your grade in this course uses criteria-based assessment rather than assignment-based grading. I am not collecting a stack of individually graded papers, quizzes, or exercises and averaging them. Instead, you are assessed on whether you meet a set of explicit, predetermined criteria between your attendance, in-class participation, and your final portfolio. You will submit work as we go, but I will only comment on it, not grade it. You can revise as much as you like before submission of your final portfolio, and you will get all points for each piece based if you have adequately completed the assignment and met the criteria of the task.

- **Attendance** — up to 3 points
- **Portfolio** — up to 14 points
- **In-Class Participation** — up to 3 points

These 20 points convert directly to your final letter grade for the course:

| Points | Grade |
| ------ | ----- |
| 18–20  | A     |
| 16–17  | B     |
| 14–15  | C     |
| 12–13  | D     |
| 0–11   | F     |
## I. Attendance

Regular attendance is required and is treated as a baseline condition of the course, not an optional courtesy. This is not merely administrative: class attendance is, across the empirical literature, the single strongest predictor of final grades in college courses — a stronger predictor than SAT scores, high school GPA, or self-reported study habits. Mandatory attendance policies show a modest but real positive effect on grades and disproportionately benefit students who are otherwise at risk of failing.

**Policy:**

- Fewer than three absences: 3 points added to your final grade.
- Three to five absences: 2 points added to your final grade.
- More than five absences: 0 points added, and the final course grade is capped at a C, regardless of performance on other coursework.
- Absences connected to a documented disability, religious observance, medical emergency, or university-sanctioned activity are handled through accommodation and do not count against the above thresholds; raise these with the instructor as early as possible.

**Punctuality:** Students are expected to be logged in, camera-ready, and prepared to begin at the scheduled start time. Arriving more than 15 minutes late on three occasions will be treated as one unexcused absence.
## 2. Portfolio

Students assemble a final portfolio from work produced across the semester, revised in light of later units. The portfolio contains:

1. A sentence-level exercise (Week 2), revised using Week 12–13 editing principles.
2. A generative paragraph (Week 3), revised for coherence.
3. A claim-and-evidence paragraph (Weeks 5–8), 
4. The full structured essay (drafted Week 10, revised Weeks 11–13), as the centerpiece.
5. A reflective cover letter (Week 14) explaining what changed across drafts and why.

## Portfolio Grading

Each piece is checked against one plain standard and is either complete (full points) or not yet complete (0 points, revise and resubmit by Week 14, no penalty). Pieces are not scored on a quality scale or averaged.

| Portfolio Piece                 | Specifications for “Complete”                                           | Points |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| 1. Sentence-level exercise      | Three imitation sentences, revised once.                                | 2      |
| 2. Generative paragraph         | One paragraph with a topic sentence and developed subordination.        | 2      |
| 3. Claim-and-evidence paragraph | A specific claim supported by one integrated quotation and analysis.    | 3      |
| 4. Full structured essay        | A complete, revised essay with a sustained claim and visible structure. | 4      |
| 5. Reflective cover letter      | A short letter connecting the four pieces to the course's threads.      | 3      |

**Portfolio total:  14 points.**

## 3. Participation

Up to 3 points for in-class engagement: coming prepared having done the reading, contributing to discussion, and participating in in-class exercises (including Week 1). This is assessed holistically at the end of the semester, not tallied class by class. If you reach out, I can give you a sense of how you are doing. Ideally you would make an informed contribution or thoughtful question once a week.
