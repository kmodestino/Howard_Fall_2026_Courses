## Today's Assignment: Structural Outline

- Due **today at 6:30 p.m.** Same submission procedure as always. Instructions are on the course website.
- Use the rest of class to do it. It should be quick, so don't belabor it.
- A **structural outline** (or **reverse outline**) gives **one line per paragraph** saying what that paragraph **does**: its **job**, not its content.
    - Examples of jobs: states the claim; presents the strongest evidence; raises and answers a counterargument; closes by returning to an opening image.
    - It's an x-ray of your essay. It shows you the essay's shape.
- Your essay is still a work in progress, and the outline should reflect that.
    - If you only have one paragraph so far, still do this.
    - Some lines will describe paragraphs you've written. Others will describe paragraphs you expect or hope to write.
    - **Speculation is fine, even useful.** Guessing at a paragraph's job now is how you find out whether the essay needs it.
    - If you're unsure about a paragraph, write your best guess and mark it with a **question mark**.
- Aim for roughly **6 to 10 lines**. Ten would be long for a five-page paper, but it's fine to include extra ideas now, since you may cut later.

## On the Writing Process

- I'm assigning this now so you think about the whole essay while there's still plenty of time before the draft is due.
- Don't write the essay at the last minute. I did that as a college student and regretted it; learning not to was a huge improvement in the quality of my work and my life.
- What works better: **write about it 10 minutes a day.** Don't tell yourself you have to draft pages at once. Tell yourself you just need a couple of sentences today.
- When a writing project is **active**, something you return to daily or nearly every day, it stays fresh. Ideas come to you in between sessions, you reassess as you go, and it feels less like pulling teeth and more like a natural process of thinking something through.
- Spacing out the work gives you more mental stamina and more ideas than a 1 a.m. rush.
- Good essays are usually **overwritten first**. Getting the good essay out is a matter of editing and cutting. Most of what I write begins at about twice the length of the final version.
- These short assignments are meant to force some of that spacing out. Eventually you'll need to do it on your own.
