## Key Outcomes

The class analyzed the structural and rhetorical techniques used in Jonathan Edwards' 1741 sermon to evoke fear and urgency in his congregation 12. Students explored the difference between coordinate and subordinate paragraph structures and identified specific rhetorical devices such as anaphora and polysyndeton 345.

## Rhetorical Concepts & Analysis

- Paragraph Structures
    
    - Coordinate Paragraphs: A structure where a topic sentence is followed by several independent units that amplify the main image; these units can often be reordered without losing logical sense 3.
    - Subordinate Paragraphs: A hierarchical structure where each sentence depends on the previous one to build a complex, graduated metaphor 46.
- Specific Rhetorical Devices
    
    - Polysyndeton: The use of excessive or grammatically "extravagant" coordinating conjunctions (e.g., "and yet") to mimic speech rhythms or highlight paradoxes 57.
    - Asyndeton: The intentional omission of conjunctions to increase the speed and urgency of the prose 89.
    - Anaphora: The repetition of a word or phrase at the beginning of successive sentences to provide emphasis 10.

## Sermon Context & Theology

- Calvinist Pillars: The sermon relies on the concepts of total depravity (sinners have no standing before God) and divine sovereignty (existence depends solely on God's arbitrary will) 11.
- Rhetorical Goal: To dismantle the "presumption" of the congregation—the false belief that their own goodness or general providence keeps them from hell 2.
- Historical Context: Delivered during the First Great Awakening, the sermon used concrete, visceral imagery rather than abstract theological arguments to provoke a physical reaction from listeners 1213.
