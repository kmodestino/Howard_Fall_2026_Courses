# The Unfinished Errand

### Writing in the Tradition of American Dissent

Every argument that has ever landed started out as a sentence. This course is built the same way, moving one skill at a time from the smallest unit of writing to the largest: sentence, paragraph, claim, evidence and analysis, structure, rhetorical choice, and editing. Each unit builds on the last, so by the end of the semester you can put together a full essay that actually holds up under pressure, not just fills the required page count.

To learn each skill, we read writers who had to get it right the first time — Winthrop, Douglass, Walker, Apess, Truth, and others who used writing to hold America accountable to the ideals it claimed for itself. You are not here to become a specialist in early American literature, though you will pick some up along the way. You are here because these writers are unusually good teachers of craft: how a sentence is built, how a claim is made to stick, how evidence is put to work, how an essay is structured to survive an objection. We will read each piece with a craft question in mind, then you will try the same move yourself, on your own material.

By December, you'll have a portfolio built the same way these texts were: sentence by sentence, revised on purpose, aimed at something you actually wanted to say.
