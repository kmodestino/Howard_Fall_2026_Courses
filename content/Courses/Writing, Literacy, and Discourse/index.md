## **Our Approach to Writing, Literacy and Discourse: Critical Algorithmic Literacy**##

This course navigates the evolving relationship between writing, literacy, and the structural power of automated systems. Rather than viewing technology as a neutral tool for expression, we will build our **Critical Algorithmic Literacy**. We define this framework as the ability to move beyond functional use to an interrogation of the socio-ethical implications of Artificial Intelligence and social media algorithms. Throughout the semester, we will investigate how AI is not merely a technical advancement but a structural force that replicates historical biases and reshapes our attention. By decoding the opaque logic of these systems, we situating ourselves as active investigators rather than passive users.

Our intellectual trajectory is anchored by two foundational texts that navigate the tension between digital productivity and social justice. We situate our study first in Ruha Benjamin’s _Race After Technology_, where we will examine the "New Jim Code" and the ways in which discriminatory designs are often baked into "objective" algorithms (Benjamin, 2019). We balance this systemic critique with Jenny Odell’s _How to Do Nothing_, which offers a method for reclaiming our attention from the "attention economy" and resisting the techno-determinism that dictates modern life (Odell, 2019). Together, these works allow us to interrogate how the labor of refusal can be a radical tool for reclaiming intellectual agency in an automated world.

To ground these theories in the present moment, we will navigate a series of case studies involving the **Big Four** AI models: **Gemini, Llama, ChatGPT, and Claude**. We will critically interrogate the mission statements of Google, Meta, OpenAI, and Anthropic to decode the gap between corporate rhetoric and the material realities of their algorithmic impact. By comparing how these companies define their goals to critical interventions into the political economies of tech. we will build a "living archive" that documents how language is used to mediate power in the age of automation.
